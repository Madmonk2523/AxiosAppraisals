Estate and probate appraisals in New York and New Jersey

When a property is part of an estate, a clear opinion of value helps the people responsible for it take the next step. Axios Appraisals works with executors, family representatives, attorneys, and advisers on current and retrospective real estate valuations.

A valuation for the date that matters

An estate appraisal may be needed for probate administration, estate settlement, or tax-related planning. A current appraisal addresses value today; a date-of-death appraisal addresses a specified date in the past. The appropriate date and intended use should be confirmed with your attorney or tax adviser before the assignment begins.

From our Queens office, we discuss assignments across New York and New Jersey. Review our service areas or contact us with the property address to confirm coverage.

What to have ready

  • The property address, type, and your role in the estate.
  • The intended use of the report and the valuation date requested by your adviser.
  • An access contact and any relevant deadline.
  • Available records, photographs, improvements, or details about the property’s condition at the valuation date.

What to expect

  1. 01

    Discuss the assignment

    We review the property, intended use, effective date, available information, and access needs. The scope, fee, and expected schedule are discussed before work begins.

  2. 02

    Research the property

    The appraisal considers property characteristics and relevant market evidence. For a retrospective assignment, information must relate to the requested historical date, including changes made since then.

  3. 03

    Receive the report

    The report explains the valuation, supporting analysis, and assumptions. Share any questions or additional documentation with us so the assignment remains aligned with its agreed purpose.

Common questions

Can you value a property as of a date in the past?

Yes. Axios offers retrospective and date-of-death appraisals. Tell us the date your adviser requires and what records are available so we can discuss the assignment.

What if the home has changed since that date?

Tell us about renovations, damage, or other changes. Older photographs and property records can help document its earlier condition. We will discuss what information is needed.

Should I request the appraisal myself or through an attorney?

Executors, family representatives, and advisers can contact us. Identify who is commissioning the report and who needs to use it; we will clarify those details before proceeding.
